## S1 · THE MARKET PENDULUM — the problem with funnel choice · SLIDES 4-7 · fast mirror

**[SLIDE 4]** on screen: HIGH TICKET ↔ LOW TICKET / PAID ↔ ORGANIC / WEBINAR ↔ VSL / INBOUND ↔ OUTBOUND

> Most business owners in 2026 are being told some version of the same handful of things.
> High ticket is dead. Low ticket is the future. Low ticket is a distraction. You need to sell high ticket. Webinars work. Webinars are saturated.
> You need a VSL. You need content. You need a setter. You need an AI agent. You need more paid traffic. You need to stop running paid traffic and build organically.
> And none of those things are necessarily wrong. The problem is that they are normally presented as isolated answers.
> So a founder sees a model working for somebody else, pays to install it, and then discovers that they do not have that person's audience, offer, trust, margins, team, sales process, or market timing.
> The model may have worked. The conditions that made the model work did not transfer.

**[SLIDE 5 — BROKEN ECONOMICS]**

> Reach changes. Trust changes. Ad costs change. AI makes it easier for everybody to create more content, more specific content, more offers, and more noise.
> A funnel that worked twelve months ago may still technically function while the economics underneath it quietly stop working.
> And because the business was built around one fixed funnel rather than an adaptable system, the founder reacts by switching again.

**[SLIDE 6 — CHOICE]**

> Honestly, one of the worst things that ever happened in entrepreneurship was choice.
> There are so many models, channels, tactics, and experts pulling people in different directions that they never hold one thing long enough to understand why it works.
> They do not get enough velocity. They do not get enough clean data. They do not compound what they learned. They just keep changing contexts.

**[REHOOK]** > And that is where the real loop begins.

## S2 · THE THREE GATES · SLIDES 8-14 · green yes path, red no paths

**[SLIDE 8]** on screen: RIGHT MESSAGE? → RIGHT MODEL? → RIGHT JOURNEY?

> Regardless of whether you are trying to get from zero to ten thousand a month, thirty thousand a month, or break through one hundred thousand for the first time, the same three gates keep appearing.
> The symptoms change as the company grows. The loop does not.

**[SLIDE 9 — GATE ONE, RIGHT MESSAGE]**

> Does the market recognize itself in what you are saying? Do they care about the problem you are naming? Do they understand the outcome? Does the argument meet them at the level of awareness they are actually in?
> If the answer is no, the founder rewrites the hook, changes the positioning, adds another angle, changes the offer, or creates more content.

**[DRAW: red NO path to Restart]** > Then they start again.

**[SLIDE 10 — GATE TWO, RIGHT MODEL]**

> Does the way you are acquiring and converting customers fit your current resources and economics?
> If you have more money than time, paid distribution may make sense. If you have more time than money, organic content, DMs, partnerships, and manual follow-up can become the proving ground.
> High ticket, low ticket, webinars, workshops, direct booking, outbound, and organic are not religions. They are models.
> And the right model depends on the market, the margins, the sales cycle, the trust required, and what you can actually sustain.
> If the answer is no, the founder changes channels, builds another funnel, hires another agency, or swaps high ticket for low ticket.

**[DRAW: red NO path to Restart]** > Then they start again.

**[SLIDE 11 — GATE THREE, RIGHT JOURNEY]**

> Once somebody enters, does the complete journey hold their attention and move them toward the transformation?
> Does the ad continue into the page? Does the page continue into the email? Does the email continue into the training? Does the sales conversation continue the same argument? Does fulfillment continue the same transformation after they buy?
> Or does every asset begin a different sale?
> If the answer is no, the founder adds more emails, rewrites the VSL, changes the sales script, adds pressure, lowers the price, or hires a better closer.

**[DRAW: red NO path to Restart]** > Then they start again.

**[SLIDE 12 — FULL THREE-GATE LOOP]**

> By the time they solve the third problem, the first one has changed.
> By the time they fix acquisition, the new channel brings in a different level of awareness.
> By the time they rewrite the VSL, sales is still using the old argument.
> By the time they improve closing, fulfillment is promising something else.
> They make local progress. The whole system never compounds.

**[MICRO-HOOK]** > That repeated return to zero has a name.

## S3 · THE INVISIBLE DOOR AND THE RESTART · SLIDES 15-18 · slow down

**[SLIDE 15 — THE INVISIBLE DOOR]**

> We call the hidden gap between those assets The Invisible Door. It is the point where the prospect leaves one touchpoint and enters the next.
> Most businesses treat each asset as a separate object. An ad. A landing page. A VSL. A webinar. An application. A sales call.
> But the prospect experiences one continuous decision.
> And every time the next touchpoint changes the problem, the promise, the mechanism, the language, or the action, the prospect has to reorient.

**[SLIDE 16 — THE RESTART]**

> That is The Restart.
> The Restart is what happens when an incongruent touchpoint breaks the argument established before it, resets the prospect's understanding, and forces the funnel to compete against itself.
> Your funnel starts fighting itself. The ad earns attention, but the page spends it. The training builds belief, but the sales call starts discovery from zero. The sale creates one expectation, and onboarding introduces another.
> The founder then has to personally reconnect all the pieces every time.

**[SLIDE 17 — RESTART CYCLE]**

> So the loop becomes: build something. Run it briefly. Get an unclear result. Change the model. Lose the previous learning. Start again. And again. And again.

**[REHOOK]** > The way out is not to become perfect at three disconnected systems. The way out is to install one central system that connects all three.

## S7 · CONSUMPTION IS PART OF THE PRODUCT · SLIDES 33-37

**[SLIDE 33 — DELIVERY VS MOVEMENT]**

> Most funnels measure whether somebody opted in. The Roadmap System measures whether they actually moved.
> A good resource does not create value when it is delivered. It creates value when it is consumed, understood, and acted on.
> So consumption cannot be an afterthought. It has to be designed into the product.

**[SLIDE 34 — CONSUMPTION ENGINE]**

> That is where the checkpoints, visible progress, meaningful stakes, personalization, and useful unlocks come in.
> Not childish gamification. Not fake scarcity. Not an arbitrary timer attached to a worthless PDF.

**[SLIDE 35 — THE CATALYST]**

> The person earns it by completing the meaningful checkpoints.

**[SLIDE 36 — 72 HOURS]**

## S8 · PERSONALIZATION AND USEFUL ACCOUNTABILITY · SLIDES 38-40

**[SLIDE 38 — RESPONSE DATA]**

> The Roadmap can collect the person's goal, target, timeline, current constraint, failed solution, awareness level, and the actions they say they are willing to take. That means the follow-up can reflect their reality.
> A long time ago, I took somebody's long-term goal, broke it down into the daily input required, and followed up with a simple question: "How is that X per day going?"
> The reply rate was good because it was specific. It was based on their goal. It did not feel like a manufactured sales touch.

**[SLIDE 39 — EXAMPLE]**

> If somebody says they need thirty qualified conversations this month, the follow-up can ask: "How are those two conversations a day going?"
> That is not arbitrary urgency. It is relevant accountability attached to what they said they wanted.

**[SLIDE 40 — AGREEMENT TRACKING]**

> And as people move through the system, you can see whether they agree with the problem, the mechanism, the solution, and the product or implementation path.
> You can see where they stop. Where they disagree. What they misunderstand. What questions keep appearing.
> That is where the Roadmap starts becoming self-proving.

## S14 · HOW WE INSTALL IT — customise the argument, not the structure · camera + overlay cards

> The structure can be templated. The argument cannot.
> We do not take a generic script, replace the company name, and call it a proprietary funnel.
> Before the Roadmap is built, we build the foundational argument underneath it. We get inside the head of the buyer.
> What do they comfortably say they want? And what is the private reason underneath that? The thing they think about when they are lying in bed, staring at the ceiling, and nobody else is there to hear it.
> We map the visible symptoms, the root problem, the failed solutions, the new mechanism, the required beliefs, the desired identity, and the honest path to the outcome.
> Then we create a strong first draft.
> B2B is not written exactly like B2C. Health is not written exactly like wealth or relationships. A sophisticated niche market is not written like a broad consumer market.
> And selling to men or selling to women may require different language, framing, emotional weighting, and front-end naming without reducing anybody to a stereotype.
> We have templated sections, scripts, assets, and matrices to do the heavy lifting.
> Then we go through the entire thing with the client. What sounds like you? What would you never say? Where do you naturally explain it better? What feels untrue? What needs proof?
> We refine it until it feels like the strongest, clearest version of what they already believe.
> Then the market makes it better. We write the first complete version. The client makes it true to their voice. The market makes it increasingly resonant.
